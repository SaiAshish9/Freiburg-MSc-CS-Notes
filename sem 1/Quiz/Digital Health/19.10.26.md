
### **1) What sort of medical data types can mobile devices provide?**

Mobile and wearable devices capture continuous, multidomain data sources that include numerical measurements and vital signs (like heart rate, sleeping hours and blood oxygen), recorded signals and timeseries (such as ECGs and accelerometer motion data), and behavioral or exposure data.

### **2) Why is the integration of mobile data clinically relevant?**

Data integration is essential because it helps answer complex clinical questions regarding evidence-based treatments, links symptoms to specific conditions, and allows clinicians to compare outcomes and treatment costs. Furthermore, continuous wearable data enables early population-level surveillance for viral infections like COVID-19 or influenza and assists in managing chronic illnesses like COPD.

### **3) What does the exposome include? What is the behaviourome?**

The **exposome** encompasses external environmental exposures over time, including pollutants, diet, metabolites, xenobiotics, drugs, and gut flora. The **behaviourome** (or health-behaviorome) tracks human lifestyle behaviors, daily routines, physical activities, and digital interactions gathered via apps and wearables.

### **4) Please describe an example of context awareness related to healthcare?**

A prime example is automatic sleep phase detection, where apps like _Chronify_ use smartphone and wearable sensors (such as motion variance and microphone activity) to detect when a user falls asleep and calculate sleep quality without requiring manual logs. Another example is tracking daily resting heart rate trends to automatically detect physiological responses to viral infections.

### **5) What context types can smartphones provide?**

Smartphones can provide several context categories:

- **User activity:** e.g., sitting, walking, drinking, or eating.    
- **User state:** e.g., physical effort, stress levels, and heart rate.
- **Environmental state:** e.g., light levels, sound, and weather.
- **Location:** e.g., office, elevator, or lecture hall.
- **Digital activity / Background data:** e.g., social media visits, app usage, and navigation data.

### **6) What is a digital health app according to the DiGA regulation?**

According to the German **Digital Care Act (DVG)**, digital health applications (DiGA) are regulated medical apps integrated into the country's national healthcare system framework, allowing for official reimbursement and clinical use.

### **7) Which requirements need to be fulfilled to enter an app into the DiGA Registry?**

To be successfully listed in the DiGA registry, an application must pass strict evaluations regarding data privacy compliance, continuously document positive care effects through scientific studies, and meet official standards for national market distribution and store handling.

### **8) Describe the idea behind the self-certification and continuous certification?**

Self-certification and continuous certification frameworks are designed to ensure that digital health apps consistently uphold data security, interoperability standards, and demonstrated clinical efficacy over time rather than just meeting standards at initial launch. _(Note: Detailed mechanics are explored in later course modules)._

### **9) Which basic elements lead from data to medical decision support within the scope of Digital Health?**

The pipeline moves from raw data to decision support through several key layers: collecting multidomain continuous data, signal preprocessing and feature extraction (filtering and outlier removal), applying pattern recognition and AI models (classification, regression, or discovery), and using knowledge inference, digital biomarkers, or digital health twins to guide medical decisions.

### **10) How do doctors learn about digital health applications?**

Doctors typically learn about digital health applications through professional education, specialized training courses, and academic lectures covering clinical perspectives and prescription models. They also discover approved tools by referencing official healthcare registries and directories, such as the BfArM DiGA directory in Germany.

### **11) Can doctors prescribe an app?**

Yes, qualified physicians can prescribe approved digital health applications (such as DiGA apps), making digital therapeutics an integrated part of modern prescription models and clinical treatment pathways.
