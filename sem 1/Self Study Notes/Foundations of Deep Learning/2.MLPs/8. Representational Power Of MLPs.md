### 1. Universal Approximation Theorem

- **What it states:**
    - A feedforward neural network with a **single hidden layer** containing a finite number of neurons (with non-linear activation functions like Sigmoid or ReLU) can approximate any continuous function on compact subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ to **arbitrary precision ($\varepsilon > 0$)**.
    
- **What it does NOT state:**
    - **How to find the weights:** It is an existence proof, not a constructive algorithm; gradient descent is not guaranteed to find these optimal weights.
    - **Efficiency / Network size:** It does not state that the required number of hidden units will be reasonable (the layer might require an exponentially large width).
    - **Generalization:** It guarantees low approximation error on the training set/domain, but says nothing about generalization performance on unseen test data.


### 2. Representational Capacity by Depth

- **Without Hidden Layers (Linear Perceptron):**
    - Can only represent **linearly separable functions** (decision boundaries are hyperplanes).
    
- **With 1 Hidden Layer:**
    - Universal approximator for any continuous function, capable of forming **arbitrary continuous decision boundaries** (given sufficient width).
    
- **With Many Hidden Layers (Deep Networks):**
    - Can represent **complex compositional and hierarchical abstractions** with exponentially fewer total parameters/neurons compared to a shallow 1-hidden-layer network.
