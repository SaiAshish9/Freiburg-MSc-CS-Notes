## 1. What are typical examples of activation functions?

- **ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit):**
    
    - **Formula:** $\text{ReLU}(x) = \max(0, x)$
    - **Characteristics:** Extremely popular due to computational efficiency and mitigation of the vanishing gradient problem in positive regions; suffers from the "dying ReLU" problem for negative inputs.
    
- **Sigmoid (Logistic):**
  
    - **Formula:** $\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}$  
    - **Characteristics:** Squashes input values to a range between $(0, 1)$, historically used for binary classification output layers, but prone to vanishing gradients.
    
- **Tanh (Hyperbolic Tangent):**
      
    - **Formula:** $\tanh(x) = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}}$
    - **Characteristics:** Similar to the sigmoid function, but zero-centered, mapping inputs to the range $(-1, 1)$.    
    
- **Leaky ReLU / Parametric ReLU (PReLU):**
    
    - **Formula:** $\text{Leaky ReLU}(x) = \max(\alpha x, x)$ where $\alpha$ is a small constant (e.g., $0.01$).
    - **Characteristics:** Introduces a small, non-zero gradient for negative inputs to prevent dying neurons.
      
    
- **Softmax:**
    
    - **Formula:** $\sigma(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j} e^{z_j}}$    
    - **Characteristics:** Generalization of logistic regression for multi-class classification, converting a vector of raw scores into a probability distribution summing to 1.

## 2. What is the purpose of using non-linear activation functions?

- **Universal Approximation Capability:** Without non-linearities, stacking multiple linear layers (matrix multiplications) collapses mathematically into a **single linear transformation** ($W_2(W_1x) = (W_2W_1)x$). Non-linear activation functions allow neural networks to approximate highly complex, non-linear functions and decision boundaries.
- **Feature Representation Learning:** They enable networks to learn intricate, hierarchical representations of data (e.g., curved shapes, text semantics, or complex image patterns) that linear models cannot capture.
- **Multi-Layer Depth Utilization:** By introducing non-linear transformations between layers, deep neural networks can genuinely leverage their depth, where each subsequent layer builds increasingly abstract feature abstractions from the previous layer's outputs.
