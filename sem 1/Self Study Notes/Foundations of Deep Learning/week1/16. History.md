
We're in the third wave of popularity of neural networks

How information processing works in the brain

1. Dendrites input information to the cell 
2. Neuron fires (has action potential) if a certain threshold for the voltage is exceeded 
3. Output of information by axon 
4. The axon is connected to dentrites of other cells via synapses
5. Learning: adaptation of the synapse’s efficiency, its synaptical weight


What it means for a cell to fire that it releases an electric pulse that travels along this axon
towards synapses that connect to other neurons

At these synapses, there are so-called neurotransmitters that are being released and they allow for an information flow through the network of neurons.

It is hypothesized that learning actually corresponds to a change in the weight of these synapses.

When our neuron fires, this synaptic weight tells us how strongly the following neuron is affected


Deep Learning has developed in several waves 

The early days, under the name of artificial neural networks/cybernetics 
- 1943 Artificial neurons as a model of brain function [McCulloch and Pitts, 1943]
- 1949 Hebbian learning [Hebb, 1949] 
- 1958 Rosenblatt perceptron [Rosenblatt, 1958] 
- 1960 Adaline → stochastic gradient descent [Widrow and Hoff, 1960] 

The first time the popularity of NNs declined:
- Negative result: linear models cannot represent the XOR function 
- Backlash against biologically inspired learning [Minsky and Papert, 1969

- 1980 - early 2000s (under the name of connectionism) 
- 1980 Neocognitron [Fukushima and Miyake, 1980] (used for character recognition)
- 1986 Multilayer Perceptrons and backpropagation [Rumelhart et al., 1986] 
- 1989 Autoencoders [Baldi and Hornik, 1989], Convolutional neural networks [LeCun et al., 1989] 
- 1997 LSTMs [Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, 1997] 
 
The second time the popularity of NNs declined:
- Ventures based on NNs made unrealistically ambitious claims
	- AI research could not fulfill these unreasonable expectations 
- Other fields of machine learning made advances 
	- E.g., SVMs and graphical models 
	- SVMs worked really well
	- SVMs were the state of the art on many datasets (data was small), specialized ConvNets held state of the art on MNIST but didn’t scale

History of Deep Learning and ANNs (cont.) 

Mid 2000s, the field got re-invigorated: 
1. Greedy layer-wise pretraining [Hinton et al., 2006] 
	1. It was now possible to train much deeper networks 
2. Several groups “resurrected” the idea of training large neural networks supervisedly using large amounts of data. 
	1. Most prominently, [Krizhevsky et al., 2012] improved results on Imagenet dataset benchmark by large margin (revolutionised computer vision)
3. Since then: exponential growth 
	1. NeurIPS attendance has grown exponentially 
		1. In 2018, it sold out in 12 minutes; they then set up a lottery system 
	2. Some people are raising unrealistic expectations 
	3. Let’s see how long this current wave persists
