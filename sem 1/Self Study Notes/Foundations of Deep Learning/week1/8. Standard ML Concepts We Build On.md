Logistic Regression:
- We can write prediction as a function of weights w and the loss function that we use in order to optimize these weights.

This is precisely the most popular loss function that we also use in deep learning.

## Logistic Regression Functions (Goodfellow et al., p. 107)

### 1. Hypothesis Function
$$h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x}) = g(w_0 + w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2)$$

### 2. Weight Vector
$$\mathbf{w} = \begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

### 3. Loss Function (Binary Cross-Entropy)
$$J(h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x}), y) = \begin{cases} -\log(h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})) & \text{for } y = 1 \\ -\log(1 - h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})) & \text{for } y = 0 \end{cases}$$

# Linear Regression: Solved Examples (L2 Loss & Normal Equations)

## 1. Formulating Prediction & L2 Loss
* **Concept:** Expressing model predictions and Mean Squared Error (L2 loss) as a function of weights.

### Problem Statement
Given a dataset with a single data point $(x_1 = 2, y_1 = 5)$, define the linear prediction model and formulate the L2 loss function as a function of weight $w$.

### Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Prediction Function:**
   $$\hat{y} = h_w(x) = w \cdot x \implies \hat{y}_1 = 2w$$

2. **L2 Loss Function (Squared Error for a single point):**
   $$J(w) = \frac{1}{2} (h_w(x_1) - y_1)^2$$

3. **Substitution & Expansion:**
   $$J(w) = \frac{1}{2} (2w - 5)^2 = 2w^2 - 10w + 12.5$$

## 2. Solving via Normal Equations
* **Concept:** Finding the optimal weight vector $\mathbf{w}$ analytically using the normal equation:
  $$\mathbf{w} = (\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X})^{-1} \mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{y}$$

### Problem Statement
Given two data points with a bias term:
* Point 1: $(x = 0, y = 1)$
* Point 2: $(x = 2, y = 3)$

Find the optimal weight vector $\mathbf{w} = \begin{bmatrix} w_0 \\ w_1 \end{bmatrix}$.

### Step-by-Step Solution
1. **Design Matrix $\mathbf{X}$ and Target Vector $\mathbf{y}$:**
   $$\mathbf{X} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

2. **Transpose $\mathbf{X}^T$:**
   $$\mathbf{X}^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

3. **Compute $\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X}$:**
   $$\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$

4. **Compute Inverse $(\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X})^{-1}$:**
   * Determinant = $(2)(4) - (2)(2) = 4$
   * $(\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{X})^{-1} = \frac{1}{4} \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$

5. **Compute $\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{y}$:**
   $$\mathbf{X}^T \mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}$$

6. **Solve for $\mathbf{w}$:**
   $$\mathbf{w} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 - 3 \\ -2 + 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

### Final Answer
* Intercept ($w_0$) = $1$
* Slope ($w_1$) = $1$
* **Learned Model:** $\hat{y} = 1 + 1x$
