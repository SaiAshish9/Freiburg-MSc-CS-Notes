### 1. Tracking KL Divergence vs. Cross-Entropy Loss

#### **Question:**

> _Why would it be interesting to track the KL divergence between $p_{\text{model}}$ and $p_{\text{data}}$, rather than the typically-used cross-entropy loss?_ _(Hint: think about how close to optimal the prediction already is.)_

#### **Solution & Key Insight:**

The **Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence** measures the _excess information loss_ caused by using our model distribution $p_{\text{model}}$ to approximate the true data distribution $p_{\text{data}}$:

$$D_{\text{KL}}(p_{\text{data}} \parallel p_{\text{model}}) = H(p_{\text{data}}, p_{\text{model}}) - H(p_{\text{data}})$$

- **Limitation of Cross-Entropy Loss ($H(p_{\text{data}}, p_{\text{model}})$):** Cross-entropy measures total uncertainty, which includes the intrinsic noise of the dataset itself, represented by the Shannon entropy $H(p_{\text{data}})$. Because $H(p_{\text{data}})$ is a fixed, non-zero constant for non-deterministic data, cross-entropy can never reach $0$ even for a perfect model. This makes it difficult to assess how close the model is to the theoretical minimum achievable loss.
- **Advantage of Tracking KL Divergence ($D_{\text{KL}}$):** By subtracting $H(p_{\text{data}})$, KL divergence isolates model error from irreducible data noise. Since $D_{\text{KL}}(p_{\text{data}} \parallel p_{\text{model}}) \ge 0$, a value of **$0$** indicates exact convergence to the true data distribution. Tracking $D_{\text{KL}}$ directly tells us how much theoretical capacity remains for performance improvement.

### 2. Logistic Regression Loss as a Special Case of General Cross-Entropy

#### **Question:**

> _Show that the formula_
> $$L(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \{y_n \log \hat{y}_n + (1 - y_n) \log(1 - \hat{y}_n)\}$$
> _derived for the negative log-likelihood of Bernoulli predictions in logistic regression is a special case of the general form_
> 
> $$H(P, Q) = -\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim P} [\log Q(\mathbf{x})]$$
> _of cross-entropy._

#### **Proof / Derivation:**

**Step 1: General Definition of Discrete Cross-Entropy** For discrete distributions $P$ (true distribution) and $Q$ (model distribution), cross-entropy is defined as:

$$H(P, Q) = -\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim P} [\log Q(\mathbf{x})] = -\sum_{x} P(x) \log Q(x)$$

**Step 2: Map Distributions to Binary Logistic Regression** For a single binary sample $n$ where the true target is $y_n \in \{0, 1\}$ and the model predicts probability $\hat{y}_n \in (0, 1)$:

- **True Distribution ($P$):** A Bernoulli distribution with $P(y_n = 1) = y_n$ and $P(y_n = 0) = 1 - y_n$.
- **Model Distribution ($Q$):** A Bernoulli distribution with $Q(y_n = 1) = \hat{y}_n$ and $Q(y_n = 0) = 1 - \hat{y}_n$.


**Step 3: Evaluate Expectation for Single Data Point** Expanding the expectation over the support $\{0, 1\}$:

$$\begin{aligned} -\mathbb{E}_{y \sim P} [\log Q(y)] &= -\left[ P(y=1)\log Q(y=1) + P(y=0)\log Q(y=0) \right] \\ &= -\left[ y_n \log \hat{y}_n + (1 - y_n)\log(1 - \hat{y}_n) \right] \end{aligned}$$

**Step 4: Empirical Expectation Over $N$ i.i.d. Samples** For a dataset of $N$ independent training samples, the true population distribution $P$ is approximated by the **empirical distribution**, where the expectation $\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim P}$ becomes the sample average $\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N$:

$$L(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \left\{ y_n \log \hat{y}_n + (1 - y_n) \log(1 - \hat{y}_n) \right\}$$



## 1. Cross-Entropy

- Measures the difference between true and predicted probability distributions.
- Commonly used as a loss function in classification.
- Lower cross-entropy indicates better predictions.

Formula:

$$  
H(P,Q) = -\sum_x P(x)\log Q(x)  
$$

## 2. KL Divergence (Kullback-Leibler)

- Measures how much one probability distribution differs from another.
- Always non-negative.
- Zero when both distributions are identical.
- Not symmetric: $D_{KL}(P|Q) \neq D_{KL}(Q|P)$.

Formula:

$$  
D_{KL}(P|Q) = \sum_x P(x)\log\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}  
$$

Relationship:

$$  
H(P,Q) = H(P) + D_{KL}(P|Q)  
$$

## 3. Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

- Statistical method to find parameters that maximize the likelihood of observed data.
- Finds model parameters that make training data most probable.

Formula:

$$  
\hat{\theta}_{MLE} = \arg\max_\theta P(D|\theta)  
$$

### Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL)

- Instead of maximizing likelihood, minimize its negative logarithm.
    

$$  
NLL = -\log P(D|\theta)  
$$

## Key Relationships

- Maximizing Likelihood = Minimizing NLL.
- Minimizing Cross-Entropy = Minimizing KL Divergence (when true entropy is fixed).
- Cross-Entropy is widely used in classification.
- KL Divergence is used in VAEs and knowledge distillation.
