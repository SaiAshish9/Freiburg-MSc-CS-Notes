Relation between Kullback-Leibler diversion, Cross Entropy and Maximum Likelihood (ML) Estimation
![[Pasted image 20261105180233.png]]

## 1. Cross-Entropy

- Measures the difference between true and predicted probability distributions.
- Commonly used as a loss function in classification.
- Lower cross-entropy indicates better predictions.

Formula:

$$  
H(P,Q) = -\sum_x P(x)\log Q(x)  
$$

## 2. KL Divergence (Kullback-Leibler)

- Measures how much one probability distribution differs from another.
- Always non-negative.
- Zero when both distributions are identical.
- Not symmetric: $D_{KL}(P|Q) \neq D_{KL}(Q|P)$.

Formula:

$$  
D_{KL}(P|Q) = \sum_x P(x)\log\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}  
$$

Relationship:

$$  
H(P,Q) = H(P) + D_{KL}(P|Q)  
$$

## 3. Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

- Statistical method to find parameters that maximize the likelihood of observed data.
- Finds model parameters that make training data most probable.

Formula:

$$  
\hat{\theta}_{MLE} = \arg\max_\theta P(D|\theta)  
$$

### Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL)

- Instead of maximizing likelihood, minimize its negative logarithm.
    

$$  
NLL = -\log P(D|\theta)  
$$

## Key Relationships

- Maximizing Likelihood = Minimizing NLL.
- Minimizing Cross-Entropy = Minimizing KL Divergence (when true entropy is fixed).
- Cross-Entropy is widely used in classification.
- KL Divergence is used in VAEs and knowledge distillation.
