# Linear Regression Assumptions & Counter-Examples

>[!abstract] Overview
>To derive a valid analytical solution for the linear regression problem, specific statistical assumptions must hold regarding the error/residual term ($\varepsilon_i$)[cite: 4].

---

## 1. The Three Analytical Assumptions

1. **Zero Mean of Residuals:** The expected value of the residuals is zero across all observations:
   $$\forall i : E(\varepsilon_i) = 0$$
2. **Homoscedasticity and Independence:** The residuals are uncorrelated with each other and share a constant variance $\sigma^2$:
   $$\forall i : \text{Var}(\varepsilon_i) = \sigma^2$$
3. **Normality of Residuals:** The error terms follow a normal distribution with mean zero and variance $\sigma^2$:
   $$\forall i : \varepsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$$

---

## 2. Counter-Examples for These Assumptions

>[!question] Ponder: Counter-Examples
>Can you think of counter-examples for these assumptions[cite: 4]?

### Counter-Example 1: Zero Mean Violation ($E(\varepsilon_i) \neq 0$)
* **Scenario:** Predicting house prices using only square footage while omitting neighborhood desirability (omitted variable bias).
* **Violation:** The model systematically underpredicts prices for mansions in luxury areas and overpredicts for identical-sized homes in lower-cost areas, meaning the expected value of the residuals within specific subsets is non-zero.

### Counter-Example 2: Heteroscedasticity & Autocorrelation Violation ($\text{Var}(\varepsilon_i) \neq \sigma^2$)
* **Heteroscedasticity Scenario:** Modeling weekly food expenditure based on household income.
* **Violation:** Low-income families have tight budgets with very little variance in grocery spending, whereas high-income families exhibit massive variance (some buy basic, some buy gourmet). Thus, the error variance grows with income rather than remaining constant.
* **Autocorrelation Scenario:** Predicting daily outdoor temperature.
* **Violation:** If the model underpredicts today due to an unmeasured weather shift, it will likely underpredict tomorrow too, making consecutive residuals correlated over time.

### Counter-Example 3: Normality Violation ($\varepsilon_i \not\sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$)
* **Scenario:** Modeling insurance claim payouts or individual income.
* **Violation:** Most policyholders file zero or tiny claims, while a small fraction file extremely large claims. This creates a severe right-skewed, heavy-tailed error distribution rather than a symmetric Gaussian bell curve.
