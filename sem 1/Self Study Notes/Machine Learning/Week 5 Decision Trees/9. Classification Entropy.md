### Classification: Impurity = Entropy

Relates **KL divergence** from a uniform ("worst-case") distribution to **entropy** as an impurity measure for discrete outcomes.

#### 1. KL Divergence and Entropy
For class/outcome probabilities $p(v_k)$ ($k = 1, \dots, K$) and worst-case uniform distribution $q(v_k) = \frac{1}{K}$:

$$\text{KL}(p, q) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} p(v_k) \log_2 \left(\frac{p(v_k)}{q(v_k)}\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} p(v_k) \log_2 \left(\frac{p(v_k)}{\frac{1}{K}}\right) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} p(v_k) \log_2(p(v_k)) + \log_2(K) \sum_{k=1}^{K} p(v_k)$$

* Since $\sum_{k=1}^{K} p(v_k) = 1$, this equals $\log_2(K) - \text{H}(V)$, so maximizing divergence from uniform is equivalent to minimizing entropy:

$$\max \text{KL}(p, q) = \min \left(-\text{KL}(p, q)\right) = \min \text{H}(V)$$

---

#### 2. Definition of Entropy
For a discrete random variable $V$ with $K$ outcomes $v_k$ and probabilities $p(v_k)$ ($k = 1, \dots, K$):

$$\text{H}(V) = -\sum_{k=1}^{K} p(v_k) \log_2 p(v_k)$$

* Measures uncertainty/impurity in **bits** (base-2 logarithm).
* Uses $\lim_{x \to 0} x \log_2(x) = 0$ for zero-probability outcomes.

---

#### 3. Boolean Variable Examples ($K = 2$, outcomes $v_1, v_2$)
* **Equal split ($p(v_1) = 0.5, p(v_2) = 0.5$):**

$$\text{H}(V) = -0.5 \log_2(0.5) - 0.5 \log_2(0.5) = 1$$

* **Pure split ($p(v_1) = 0, p(v_2) = 1$):**

$$\text{H}(V) = -\lim_{x \to 0} x \log_2(x) - 1 \log_2(1) = 0$$

* Across $p(v_1) \in [0, 1]$, $\text{H}(V)$ forms a concave curve peaking at $1$ when $p(v_1) = 0.5$ and hitting $0$ at the pure endpoints $0$ and $1$.
