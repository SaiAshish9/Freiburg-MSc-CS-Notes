### Classification Trees

* **Same principle as regression trees:** Recursively partitions feature space ($x_1, x_2$, etc.) starting from root $\mathcal{S}_0$ into subsets ($\mathcal{S}_1, \mathcal{S}_2$, etc.) to route and classify query points (`?`).
* **Leaf model:** Outputs **majority vote** or the **class probability distribution** $p(c \mid v)$ over samples in the leaf.
* **Split criterion:** Selects splits maximizing purity via **Gini index**, **information gain**, or **variance reduction**.

**Classification trees** are decision tree models used when the target variable is categorical (discrete class labels) rather than continuous. Like regression trees, they partition the feature space into regions through a sequence of binary splits, routing new inputs from the root down to a leaf node to make predictions.

### Intuition on Classification Purity

Shows how candidate splits reshape child class distributions relative to the unsplit root node.

#### 1. Before Split (`a`)
* **Class distribution:** Four classes (red, green, blue, yellow/olive) appear in roughly equal proportions ($\approx 0.25$ each) across the 2D feature space.
* **High impurity:** A balanced four-way mix corresponds to high uncertainty/impurity before splitting.

---

#### 2. Split 1 — Horizontal Cut (`b`)
* **Partition:** A horizontal dashed line divides the data into **top** and **bottom** subsets.
* **Child distributions:**
  * **Top:** Mix of red ($\approx 0.2$), blue ($\approx 0.3$), and yellow/olive ($\approx 0.5$); no green.
  * **Bottom:** Mix of red ($\approx 0.3$), green ($\approx 0.5$), and blue ($\approx 0.2$); no yellow/olive.
* **Takeaway:** Each child still contains **3 classes**, yielding only modest impurity reduction.

---

#### 3. Split 2 — Vertical Cut (`c`)
* **Partition:** A vertical dashed line divides the data into **left** and **right** subsets.
* **Child distributions:**
  * **Left:** Only **2 classes**—green ($\approx 0.5$) and blue ($\approx 0.5$); red and yellow/olive are absent.
  * **Right:** Only **2 classes**—red ($\approx 0.5$) and yellow/olive ($\approx 0.5$); green and blue are absent.
* **Takeaway:** Reduces active classes from 4 to 2 per side (eliminating two classes in each child), making it cleaner than Split 1 before subsequent horizontal splits isolate individual clusters.
