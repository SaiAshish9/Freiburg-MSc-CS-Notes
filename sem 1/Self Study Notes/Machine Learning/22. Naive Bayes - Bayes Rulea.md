Bayes' Rule calculates the probability of a target \(y\) given observed features \(x\), using prior probabilities and conditional probabilities.

**Definition:**

**Bayes' Rule:**

$$
P(y\mid x)=\frac{P(x\mid y)P(y)}{P(x)}
$$

**Expanded Formula:**

$$
P(y\mid x)=P(y)\frac{P(x\mid y)}{\sum_{y'\in\mathcal{Y}}P(x\mid y')P(y')}
$$

**Where:**
- $P(y\mid x)$ = Posterior probability
- $P(y)$ = Prior probability
- $P(x\mid y)$ = Likelihood
- $P(x)$ = Evidence

**Key Takeaway:** Bayes' Rule updates the probability of a target using observed evidence.

**Naive Bayes:** Extends Bayes' Rule to multiple features by assuming they are conditionally independent given the target class.



### Conditional Probability – P(B|A)

**Definition:** Probability of B occurring, given that A has occurred.

$$
P(B\mid A)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(A)}
$$

**Example:**
- Total COVID-positive patients = 4
- COVID-positive patients with fever = 3

$$
P(\text{Fever}\mid\text{COVID Positive})=\frac{3}{4}=0.75
$$

**Key Takeaway:** Divide the number of cases where both A and B occur by the total number of cases where A occurs.
