
These topics primarily focus on supervised machine learning, model training, evaluation, and improving generalization.

|Topic|Short explanation|
|---|---|
|1. Linear Methods|Models relationships using linear functions. Includes linear regression and logistic regression for prediction and classification.|
|2. Principles of Regularization|Techniques to prevent models from becoming too complex. Includes L1 (Lasso) and L2 (Ridge) regularization.|
|3. Support Vector Machines (SVM)|Classification algorithms that find a decision boundary with the maximum margin between classes. Kernels allow nonlinear boundaries.|
|4. Decision Trees|Models that make predictions using a sequence of feature-based decisions. Used for classification and regression.|
|5. Neural Networks (I)|Introduction to artificial neurons, layers, activation functions, forward propagation, and basic network architectures.|
|6. Neural Networks (II)|More advanced neural network training concepts, including backpropagation, gradient descent, and optimization.|
|7. Ensembles|Combines predictions from multiple models to improve accuracy and stability. Includes bagging, Random Forests, and boosting.|
|8. Gradient-Boosted Trees|Builds decision trees sequentially, with each new tree correcting errors of the existing model. Popular implementations include XGBoost and LightGBM.|
|9. Hyperparameter Tuning|Finds suitable model settings such as learning rate, tree depth, and regularization strength using grid search, random search, or other optimization methods.|
|10. Fighting Overfitting|Techniques to help models perform well on unseen data, including regularization, early stopping, cross-validation, and dropout.|
|11. Error Metrics|Measures model performance. Includes accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, MAE, MSE, and RMSE.|

## 5. What should you study before starting?

Focus on four foundations:

1. Linear Algebra: Vectors, matrices, matrix multiplication, dot products.
    
2. Calculus: Derivatives, partial derivatives, chain rule, gradients.
    
3. Probability & Statistics: Mean, variance, distributions, conditional probability.
    
4. Python: NumPy, Pandas, Matplotlib, and Scikit-learn.
    

Overall difficulty: Medium to High, especially if your course includes mathematical derivations and proofs. Neural Networks, SVMs, Linear Methods, and Regularization are likely to be the most mathematically demanding topics.
