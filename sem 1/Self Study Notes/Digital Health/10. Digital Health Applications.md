### 1. Symptom Tracking

Symptom tracking utilizes continuous sensor data from wearables, such as resting heart rate and sleep metrics, to serve as an early warning system for viral illnesses.

### 2. Resting Heart Rate (RHR) & Step Count

Resting heart rate (RHR) and step count measurements track physiological changes, highlighting spikes in RHR and drops in mobility during viral infections.

### 3. The Weather Issue

The weather issue accounts for seasonal fluctuations in average resting heart rate and step counts that can confound continuous health monitoring data over time.

### 4. Normalisation & Individual Baselines

Normalisation and individual baselines adjust raw sensor data to isolate true disease-related anomalies from external environmental factors like weather.

### 5. Fever Curve for Germany

The fever curve for Germany visualizes population-wide physiological detection rates derived from wearable data alongside official COVID-19 case numbers.

### 6. Wearables to Fight Pandemics

Wearables aid in combating pandemics by leveraging ubiquitous mobile and on-body sensor data for rapid symptom screening, tracking, and contact tracing.

### 7. Digital Contact Tracing & Connectivity

Digital contact tracing models when and where individuals interact by mapping daily routines, activities, and exposure locations such as homes, offices, and public transport.

### 8. Mining from Data

Data mining extracts recurring patterns, rules, and behavioral transitions from sensor time-series to model activities and state changes.

### 9. COPD Analysis

COPD stands for ==**Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease**==, a progressive lung condition that makes it hard to breathe by narrowing the airways and damaging lung tissue

COPD analysis evaluates free-living patient activity patterns—specifically time spent in moderate-to-vigorous intensity physical activity—as a key health marker for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

### 10. Augmented Human AI (A.A.I.)

Augmented Human AI explores the collaborative combination of human expertise with artificial intelligence models and inference systems to optimize decision-making.

### 11. Electronic Health Records (EHR)

Electronic Health Records (EHRs) provide a comprehensive longitudinal timeline of a patient's medical history, integrating encounters, laboratory results, medications, vital signs, notes, and diagnoses.
