The Phenome (observable health characteristics and diseases) results from interactions between the Genome (genetics) and Exposome (environmental and lifestyle exposures) across individuals and over time.

# Genome × Exposome = Phenome

## 1. Genome (Genetics)

- Complete set of genetic information (DNA) of an individual.
- Represents **what we inherit**.
- Examples:
    - Eye colour
    - Genetic predisposition to diabetes
    - Inherited disease risks

## 2. Exposome (Environment + Lifestyle)

- Total environmental and lifestyle exposures experienced throughout life.
- Represents **what we experience**.
- Examples:
    - Air pollution
    - Diet and nutrition
    - Physical activity
    - Stress
    - Smoking
    - Chemical exposure

## 3. Phenome (Observable Characteristics)

- Complete set of observable characteristics (phenotypes) of an individual.
- Represents **what we observe about a person**.
- Examples:
    - Height and weight
    - Blood pressure
    - Blood glucose levels
    - Disease conditions

## 4. Behaviourome

The **Behaviourome** (or _Behaviome_) refers to the complete collection of an individual's behaviours and behavioural patterns that influence their health.

It represents **what a person does in everyday life**.

## 2. Examples
- **Physical Activity:** Walking, running, exercising.
- **Sleep:** Sleep duration, quality and patterns.
- **Eating Habits:** Food choices and meal timings.
- **Smoking & Alcohol:** Consumption habits.
- **Social Behaviour:** Social interactions and communication.
- **Sedentary Behaviour:** Time spent sitting or being inactive.    

## 3. Behaviourome in Digital Health

Mobile and on-body systems can collect behavioural data using:

| Technology       | Behaviour Monitored                 |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Smartwatch       | Steps, physical activity, sleep     |
| Smartphone       | Mobility patterns, screen time      |
| Fitness Tracker  | Exercise frequency, activity levels |
| Sleep Tracker    | Sleep duration and patterns         |
| Wearable Sensors | Movement and sedentary behaviour    |


## 5. Relationship

$$  
\text{Genome} \times \text{Exposome} \rightarrow \text{Phenome}  
$$

- **Genome:** Genetic predisposition.
- **Exposome:** Environmental and lifestyle influences.
- **Phenome:** Resulting observable characteristics and health conditions.
- The multiplication symbol represents interaction, not literal multiplication.
    

## 5. Example: Diabetes

|Component|Example|
|---|---|
|Genome|Genetic predisposition to diabetes|
|Exposome|Unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, stress|
|Phenome|Elevated blood glucose levels or diabetes|

Two individuals with similar genetic risks can develop different health outcomes because of differences in environmental exposures and lifestyle.

## 6. Application in Digital Health

- **Smartwatches:** Track heart rate, sleep and physical activity.
- **Smartphones:** Collect lifestyle and behavioural data.
- **Wearable sensors:** Monitor physiological measurements.
- **AI & Machine Learning:** Analyse genetic, environmental and health data to support risk prediction and personalised healthcare.

## Key Takeaway

> **Genome = What you inherit**
> **Exposome = What you experience**
> **Phenome = What you exhibit**

**Digital Health Goal:** Understand how genetics and environmental exposures influence health to support personalised prevention, treatment and care.
